Will electron transfer to/from my substrate be favourable?

Estimated ΔGET
—
This is the simple Rehm–Weller estimate without the Coulombic work term (usually ≤0.1 eV in polar solvents such as MeCN), so treat it as a rough guide. Use E0,0 for the excited state that actually reacts: the singlet for most organic dyes and acridiniums, the triplet for Ir/Ru complexes and TADF triplet pathways. Slightly endergonic steps (up to ~+0.1–0.2 eV) can still work, and fast follow-up chemistry (fragmentation, deprotonation) can pull them forward.

Radical precursors, reductants and substrate classes

Oxidised precursors are listed with E(S•+/S) or E(S•/S−) for anions; reduced precursors with E(S/S•−). Most are irreversible, so the values are peak or half-peak potentials and are only approximate. The type label tells you what was measured. Ranges show the spread within a class.

Sources

    Conventions & caveats

    • Notation. E(PC•+/PC*) is the excited state acting as a reductant (more negative = stronger). E(PC*/PC•−) is the excited state acting as an oxidant (more positive = stronger). Charges are written generically; for Ru(bpy)32+, "PC•+" means Ru(III).
    • Ferrocene is the reliable link. Aqueous reference electrodes (SCE, Ag/AgCl) used in organic solvents add unknown liquid-junction potentials. IUPAC recommends reporting against Fc+/Fc measured in the same solution. Conversions to NHE or Ag/AgCl in organic solvents are approximate.
    • Fc+/Fc vs SCE depends on the solvent and electrolyte. For MeCN this tool uses 0.38 V (Pavlishchuk & Addison, 0.1 M Et4NClO4/Et4NPF6) by default, or 0.40 V (Connelly & Geiger, [NBu4][PF6]), which many organic papers use. The 20 mV difference is the real uncertainty in practice.
    • Aqueous constants used: SCE = +0.241 V vs NHE; Ag/AgCl (sat. KCl) = +0.197 V; Ag/AgCl (3 M KCl) = +0.210 V; all at 25 °C.
    • Irreversible waves. Most radical precursors fragment after electron transfer, so only a peak potential (Ep) or half-peak potential (Ep/2) is available. Ep overestimates the thermodynamic potential, often by 0.1 V or more, and depends on the scan rate. Roth, Romero & Nicewicz (2016) recommend Ep/2.
    • Thermodynamics ≠ kinetics. A favourable ΔGET means electron transfer is possible, not that it is fast. Confirm with Stern–Volmer quenching. Chain processes, EDA complexes and HAT can also give products when direct SET is uphill.
